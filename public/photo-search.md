## Latest additions

- Five views of Amenemhat’s painted tomb (BH 2), Beni Hasan. [Egypt’s Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/en/monuments/the-tomb-of-amenemhat) supplies the site context. Uneven warm lighting is visible and documented.
- Six Herculaneum views, eight from [Villa A at Oplontis](https://pompeiisites.org/en/oplontis/), and five Stabian panels. The [Stabiae excavation history](https://pompeiisites.org/en/stabiae/history-of-the-excavation/) explains the removal of painted panels for preservation. A published Herculaneum color reproduction is labeled as such.
- Three views of one [Mari Investiture painting](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010144553), plus three Urartian wall fragments at Erebuni. Mari’s date follows the current Louvre catalog, and its restored state is acknowledged. The Erebuni photographs are dark; frames and shadows should be excluded from fitting.
- Three Dunhuang images, including two silk paintings, and four Nubian murals from Faras. [Faras conservation context](https://www.mnw.art.pl/en/collections/permanent-galleries/faras-gallery/) accompanies the images. The standing Vaisravana is matched visually to [British Museum 1919,0101,0.45](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1919-0101-0-45), correcting the date/identity suggested by the Commons filename.
- Two Pazyryk textiles and a Khmer sculpture with a later lacquer coating. The latter is Angkor Wat style with unknown exact provenance; it does not fill the need for hidden-painting photographs from Angkor Wat itself.

The earlier Ice Age and early medieval collections remain: six original cave-surface views, an Azilian transition object, and 57 early medieval mural or manuscript images. Detailed acquisition records for both sweeps are retained in `data/expansions/`.

## Reuse and interpretation

New photographs were inspected alongside item-specific metadata and saved Commons license-page revisions. Original bytes are preserved. Derived comparisons, thumbnails and photographic social cards retain source-image terms and modification notices; see [CREDITS](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CREDITS.md). Published color reproductions remain explicitly identified. The separate Phrasikleia reconstruction photograph is labeled as a modern research reference, not an ancient original or a result of this app.

False color can emphasize pigment, dye, stains, repairs, weave and lighting. It cannot identify a pigment, recover an original palette, date a mark, see through opaque overpainting or establish deliberate destruction. Historical interpretation requires source context and independent evidence.

The contribution page includes a photography guide, specific requests and immediate CC BY 4.0 publication. Visitors can report rights, attribution, privacy or context concerns without a GitHub account. Reports are private to the owner.
