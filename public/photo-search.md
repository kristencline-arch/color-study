It contains 347 museum API imports, 79 additional field or archive photographs, and the original eleven studies. The preserved JPEGs total 1.64 GiB. These counts describe photographs, including distinct views, manuscript leaves and detached fragments. They are not a count of unique artifacts.

## What this iteration added

- **Asia and manuscripts:** colored Chinese and Japanese scrolls, Korean paintings, Tibetan works, Nepalese and Indian palm-leaf pages and painted covers, Mughal and Jain illuminations, Myanmar Kammavacha leaves, Kizil plaster and Indonesian textiles. Extremely thin full-scroll previews were excluded where the museum JPEG gave too little usable detail; larger museum masters remain linked on included records.
- **Macedonia:** six views at Agios Athanasios, four at the Tomb of the Palmettes, four at Nerezi and five at Kurbinovo. The [Kurbinovo site record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6492/) dates the main fresco campaign to 1191 and describes later interventions. Nerezi’s sixteenth-century Orans remains separately dated.
- **Indigenous North America and Oceania:** 22 new North American views include Tlingit and Haida painted works, Diné and Hopi textiles, and Ancestral Pueblo ceramics. Fifteen Pacific additions include Hawaiian and Fijian barkcloth, a Māori lintel, an Abelam yam mask, painted works from New Guinea and New Ireland, and a twentieth-century Arnhem Land bark painting.
- **South America:** 27 new Andean photographs include Paracas painted cotton, Nasca and Wari textiles, hats, tie-dye and painted ceramics. The cotton bundle-mask photographs show textile objects; they do not depict human remains.
- **Africa beyond Egypt:** 23 museum additions include Kuba, Dida-style, Mbuun-style and other dyed or embroidered cloth, North African textiles and painted objects. Twelve distinct leaves from the [Gunda Gunde Gospel book](https://art.thewalters.org/object/W.850.153V/) add Ethiopian illumination, writing and ornament.

## Where to look next

1. **Indigenous-led archives and fuller cultural context.** Seek collection records with community-authored names, access guidance and known makers. The current museum selection has uneven attribution and is not representative of all Indigenous peoples.
2. **South America beyond the central Andes.** Mapuche textiles, Amazonian painted objects, Ecuadorian and Colombian work, and additional original painted shelters would broaden the collection. A zero-result query is not evidence that openly licensed photographs do not exist.
3. **African manuscripts beyond this Ethiopian book.** Seek native color scans of illuminated pages from the Maghreb, the Sahel and other Ethiopian collections, with item-level image terms. The current manuscript batch contains several leaves of one book, not twelve independent manuscript traditions.
4. **More Asian cave and mural material.** Mogao, Central Asian painted fragments, Southeast Asian folding books and the faint Angkor Wat paintings remain useful directions. The [original study](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/41796676-ddf2-4326-866d-393543345e4d) and [NASA article](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Manipulating_Satellite_Photos_Now_Reveals_Ancient_Images) remain the starting references. Photographs of modern reproductions need explicit labels.
5. **Closer Egyptian and Pompeian details.** Look for matched context and detail images, even illumination, known capture dates and conservation records. Keep the existing Egypt and Rome collections growing with useful new views.
6. **Painted cliff-dwelling and kiva interiors.** Seek surviving interior plaster, with appropriate photographic access and reuse terms. [Mesa Verde’s wall-painting record](https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/education/artifactgallery_wallpainting.htm) is a lead; distant views of unpainted masonry offer little for this study.

## Selection and reuse

Each photograph includes its source, credit, item-specific license, dimensions and SHA-256 hash. The repository preserves unchanged original JPEGs and rights snapshots. Museum preview review caught two repeated object photographs; those duplicate candidates were withheld. The included library has no duplicate original hashes. Thumbnails and comparison previews are labeled derivatives.

Museum additions are CC0/public domain. Commons photographs retain their individual attribution and share-alike terms. The project’s original annotations and compilation are CC0; raw provider snapshots retain their provider terms. See [all image credits](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CREDITS.md) and [catalog documentation](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CATALOG.md).

Color separation amplifies differences already present, including paint, dye, stains, lighting and repairs. It does not reconstruct an original palette, identify pigments, see through opaque overpainting, date marks or establish why a surface was damaged.

Have a photograph of surviving paint or dyed cloth? [Contribute it to the open collection](https://color-study-painted-surfaces.kristen368163.chatgpt.site/#community) with your credit and the form’s CC BY 4.0 consent. Each record has a report link.
