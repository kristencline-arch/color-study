The collection has grown from 66 to **267 photographs**, adding 201 reviewed images. It contains 208 museum imports, 48 new field or archive photographs, and the original eleven studies. The native JPEGs total about 1.09 GiB. There are 77 textiles, and the field guide now has 50 study leads.

These counts describe photographs. They include different views of rooms and objects, detached fragments, and separate coffin parts. They are not a count of unique artifacts.

## What the wider search added

Africa beyond Egypt now includes a [Nubian painting from Faras](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faras_-_Saint_Anne_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg), Ethiopian painted panels and manuscripts, San paintings at Eland Cave, and painted panels from Tassili. The individual records in the gallery contain the exact source and license for each photograph.

South and Southeast Asia now include Sigiriya, Ajanta, Bagan, Indian painted and dyed cotton, and Indonesian cloth. East Asian additions include painted wooden masks, lacquer and devotional painting. Australian rock painting and Samoan and Hawaiian barkcloth broaden the supports and traditions represented. Their records distinguish undated living traditions and nineteenth- or twentieth-century objects from ancient art.

The Americas include Andean cloth, painted Maya ceramics and Moche architectural color at Huaca de la Luna. This search favored surviving applied color and dye over unpainted stone, ordinary architectural views and monochrome archive images.

## What is still missing

- **The faint paintings of Angkor Wat.** The [original study](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/41796676-ddf2-4326-866d-393543345e4d) and [NASA Spinoff article](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Manipulating_Satellite_Photos_Now_Reveals_Ancient_Images) remain central references. A close, native-resolution original with clear image reuse terms is still needed. A carved relief is not a substitute for the faint painted surfaces in that research.
- **More original cave murals.** Bonampak and Mogao remain strong leads. A bright, high-resolution photograph can depict a museum reproduction; one Bonampak candidate was withheld because original-versus-reproduction context could not be established confidently. Replica photographs should have their own explicit labels.
- **Painted cliff-dwelling and kiva interiors.** The target is surviving interior plaster, not a distant view of masonry. [Mesa Verde's wall-painting record](https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/education/artifactgallery_wallpainting.htm) is a useful lead. [Coronado Historic Site](https://nmhistoricsites.org/coronado/plan) prohibits visitor photography of the original Kuaua murals and painted kiva interior; an authorized archival source is needed.
- **Closer, dated Egyptian and Pompeian details.** The new room views supply context, but a small figure inside a large room photograph can still occupy few pixels. Native close-ups, capture dates, and conservation documentation would strengthen comparisons. [Getty's Nefertari project](https://www.getty.edu/projects/tomb-nefertari/) illustrates why treatment history matters.
- **Better comparative capture.** Matched context and detail photographs, even lighting, color references, and documented before-and-after conservation views are more useful than many near-identical tourist views. Separate dates and viewpoints must stay explicit.

## Selection and reuse

Every included image has a source, author, reuse license, native dimensions and SHA-256 hash. The public repository preserves the original JPEG bytes and rights snapshots. Images that were too small, monochrome, weakly relevant to surviving color, or uncertain as original surfaces were excluded from this addition. Thumbnails are orientation-corrected derivatives; image-lab comparisons are mathematical enhancements of the credited originals.

Museum additions are CC0/public domain. Field photographs retain their individual attribution and share-alike terms. Raw provider snapshots retain their provider's terms; the project's original annotations and compilation are CC0. [All image credits](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CREDITS.md) and [catalog documentation](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CATALOG.md) are available on GitHub.

Color separation can clarify differences already present in a photograph. It also amplifies stains, lighting, weave, reflections and noise. It does not reconstruct an original palette, identify pigment, reveal paint beneath opaque layers, date marks, or establish why a surface was damaged.
