## Latest additions

- Thirteen original mural views from Mexico: six from Cacaxtla, six from Bonampak and one of Teotihuacán’s Puma Mural. The new [Mesoamerica collection](https://color-study-painted-surfaces.kristen368163.chatgpt.site/?collection=mesoamerica#collection) also brings together the existing museum objects. [INAH’s Cacaxtla context](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/5417) and [Bonampak account](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/5416) accompany the studies. Two wide Cacaxtla panoramas reach 9,366 and 13,514 pixels across.
- Six Hawaiian kapa photographs and a painted paddle from Buka or north Bougainville in the Solomon Islands archipelago, present-day Papua New Guinea. Barkcloth is beaten fiber rather than woven cloth. These records identify later historic objects; two undated kapa works remain undated instead of borrowing the photograph’s date.
- Three manuscript leaves from the Mamma Haidara collection, photographed in the 2017 Paris exhibition. Their photographers’ CC BY 4.0 grants are retained separately from the public-domain artwork designation. Dates follow the exhibited-source attribution; material identifications that were not independently verified remain qualified.
- Three views of [Olowe of Ise’s palace doors](https://www.britishmuseum.org/visit/object-trails/collecting-and-empire-trail) and a Yoruba Gẹ̀lẹ̀dẹ́ mask. Surviving pigments share the surface with exposed wood, carving and shadows. The doors’ collecting history and early-20th-century date are visible in the study context.

Five candidate views were withheld: two likely or documented Bonampak reproductions, a strongly color-processed Cacaxtla mosaic, a Tepantitla view whose original-versus-copy status was unresolved, and a badly color-shifted historical print. The review record is in `data/expansions/2026-09-11-pacific-paintings.json`.

The preceding 40-image sweep remains available: Amenemhat’s tomb at Beni Hasan, Herculaneum, Oplontis, Stabiae, Mari, Erebuni, Dunhuang, Faras, Pazyryk and a Khmer sculpture with later lacquer. That sculpture is Angkor Wat style with unknown exact provenance; it is not a hidden painting from the temple itself.

The earlier Ice Age and early medieval collections remain: six original cave-surface views, an Azilian transition object, and 57 early medieval mural or manuscript images. Detailed acquisition records for both sweeps are retained in `data/expansions/`.

## Reuse and interpretation

New photographs were inspected alongside item-specific metadata and saved Commons license-page revisions. Original bytes are preserved. Derived comparisons, thumbnails and photographic social cards retain source-image terms and modification notices; see [CREDITS](https://github.com/kristencline-arch/color-study/blob/main/CREDITS.md). Published color reproductions remain explicitly identified. The separate Phrasikleia reconstruction photograph is labeled as a modern research reference, not an ancient original or a result of this app.

False color can emphasize pigment, dye, stains, repairs, weave and lighting. It cannot identify a pigment, recover an original palette, date a mark, see through opaque overpainting or establish deliberate destruction. Historical interpretation requires source context and independent evidence.

The contribution page includes a photography guide, specific requests and immediate CC BY 4.0 publication. Visitors can report rights, attribution, privacy or context concerns without a GitHub account. Reports are private to the owner.
