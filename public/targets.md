# Color Study: targets to explore

Documented means published use of decorrelation stretch. Candidate and Exploratory entries are proposed applications, not claims of discoveries.

## Angkor Wat

Cambodia · Documented

Published research used decorrelation stretch to clarify faint paintings of boats, animals, deities and buildings on the temple walls.

Photo to seek: Close, evenly lit color photographs of faded painted wall sections, including adjacent unpainted stone.

Interpretation: A carved relief and a surviving paint trace are different kinds of evidence. Match any interpretation to the original photograph.

[Tan et al., The hidden paintings of Angkor Wat, Antiquity (2014)](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-hidden-paintings-of-angkor-wat/)

## Göreme / Cappadocia

Türkiye · Candidate

Surviving red motifs and figurative frescoes in rock-hewn churches offer color information on a painted surface. The included photograph focuses on worn red decoration.

Photo to seek: Close, evenly lit original photographs of worn painted sections, with some adjacent surface.

Interpretation: Surface damage alone does not identify who caused it or why. The photograph may include conservation work.

[UNESCO, Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/357)

## Chehel Sotoun

Isfahan, Iran · Candidate

The featured high-resolution photograph includes a painted narrative scene and worn ornamental borders. Try a border fit where pigment is patchy.

Photo to seek: Worn border details and original color documentation from before and after conservation.

Interpretation: The painting is historic, not prehistoric. Brilliant areas may offer little hidden detail. Do not infer intentional erasure or original hues from false colors.

[Amir Pashaei, original photograph and metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_painting_in_Chehel_Sotoun2.jpg)

## Bhimbetka

Madhya Pradesh, India · Candidate

Surviving painted figures and faint neighboring traces make this a strong material match. A 17.9-megapixel photograph of Rock Shelter 8 is included.

Photo to seek: Close color views of the painted panel, preserving faint edges and some surrounding substrate.

Interpretation: Different paint layers, weathering and mineral staining can separate in similar ways. The app cannot date overlapping figures.

[UNESCO, Rock Shelters of Bhimbetka](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/925/)

## Greek marble sphinx

Attica, Greece / The Met · Candidate

The museum records surviving red, black and blue pigment on this original sphinx. The included open-access image supports an experiment on actual pigment traces.

Photo to seek: Close photographs of the wing, face and hair; fit to the sculpture rather than its studio backdrop.

Interpretation: Documented pigment is not a published test of this app. False colors do not reproduce the ancient palette. Avoid painted modern reconstructions as input.

[The Met, marble capital and finial in the form of a sphinx](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248501)

## Pompeii / House of the Vettii

## Beni Hasan

Egypt · Documented

A published DStretch study clarified obscure animal details in Egyptian tomb paintings, making this a direct precedent for photographic pigment enhancement.

Photo to seek: Tightly framed painted animals, outlines and faded details, with the wall evenly illuminated.

Interpretation: Compare with epigraphic records and conservation history before interpreting an unusual shape.

[Evans and Mourad, DStretch and Egyptian tomb paintings (2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X17305977)
