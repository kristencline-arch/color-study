# Color Study: painted surfaces to explore

20 sourced targets. Documented means published use of the technique; Candidate is a proposed use based on painted material, not a validated result from this app. Exploratory examples illustrate limits.

## 1. Angkor Wat (Cambodia)

Documented | Temple wall paintings

Published research used decorrelation stretch to clarify faint paintings of boats, animals, deities and buildings on the temple walls.

**Photo to seek:** Close, evenly lit color photographs of faded painted wall sections, including adjacent unpainted stone.

**Interpretation:** A carved relief and a surviving paint trace are different kinds of evidence. Match any interpretation to the original photograph.

[Tan et al., The hidden paintings of Angkor Wat, Antiquity (2014)](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-hidden-paintings-of-angkor-wat/)

## 2. Göreme / Cappadocia (Türkiye)

Candidate | Painted church interiors

Surviving red motifs and figurative frescoes in rock-hewn churches offer color information on a painted surface. The included photograph focuses on worn red decoration.

**Photo to seek:** Close, evenly lit original photographs of worn painted sections, with some adjacent surface.

**Interpretation:** Surface damage alone does not identify who caused it or why. The photograph may include conservation work.

[UNESCO, Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/357)

Included study: Cappadocia, Turkey — 3,456 × 2,304 (8.0 MP). [Original source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fresco_at_the_Dark_Church,_in_Goreme_(6).JPG).

## 3. Chehel Sotoun (Isfahan, Iran)

Candidate | Historic palace murals

The featured high-resolution photograph includes a painted narrative scene and worn ornamental borders. Try a border fit where pigment is patchy.

**Photo to seek:** Worn border details and original color documentation from before and after conservation.

**Interpretation:** The painting is historic, not prehistoric. Brilliant areas may offer little hidden detail. Do not infer intentional erasure or original hues from false colors.

[Amir Pashaei, original photograph and metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_painting_in_Chehel_Sotoun2.jpg)

Included study: Chehel Sotoun, Iran — 4,108 × 3,604 (14.8 MP). [Original source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_painting_in_Chehel_Sotoun2.jpg).

## 4. Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh, India)

Candidate | Painted rock shelters

Surviving painted figures and faint neighboring traces make this a strong material match. A 17.9-megapixel photograph of Rock Shelter 8 is included.

**Photo to seek:** Close color views of the painted panel, preserving faint edges and some surrounding substrate.

**Interpretation:** Different paint layers, weathering and mineral staining can separate in similar ways. The app cannot date overlapping figures.

[UNESCO, Rock Shelters of Bhimbetka](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/925/)

Included study: Bhimbetka, India — 5,184 × 3,456 (17.9 MP). [Original source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rock_Shelter_8,_Bhimbetka_03.jpg).

## 5. Greek marble sphinx (Attica, Greece / The Met)

Candidate | Polychrome marble sculpture

The museum records surviving red, black and blue pigment on this original sphinx. The included open-access image supports an experiment on actual pigment traces.

**Photo to seek:** Close photographs of the wing, face and hair; fit to the sculpture rather than its studio backdrop.

**Interpretation:** Documented pigment is not a published test of this app. False colors do not reproduce the ancient palette. Avoid painted modern reconstructions as input.

[The Met, marble capital and finial in the form of a sphinx](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248501)

Included study: Painted marble sphinx — 1,893 × 4,000 (7.6 MP). [Original source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248501).

## 6. Pompeii / House of the Vettii (Italy)

Candidate | Faded figurative fresco

The included Cyparissus image shows worn pigment and pale contours, providing a closer painted-surface example than a room overview.

**Photo to seek:** Straight-on high-resolution color photographs with the painting filling the frame.

**Interpretation:** Missing plaster cannot be recovered by color enhancement. Check conservation records before interpreting unusual boundaries.

[Original Cyparissus fresco photograph and metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fresco_depicting_the_metamorphosis_of_Cyparissus,_House_of_the_Vettii,_Pompeii.jpg)

Included study: House of the Vettii — 2,766 × 3,688 (10.2 MP). [Original source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fresco_depicting_the_metamorphosis_of_Cyparissus,_House_of_the_Vettii,_Pompeii.jpg).

## 7. Beni Hasan (Egypt)

Documented | Painted tomb scenes

A published DStretch study clarified obscure animal details in Egyptian tomb paintings, making this a direct precedent for photographic pigment enhancement.

**Photo to seek:** Tightly framed painted animals, outlines and faded details, with the wall evenly illuminated.

**Interpretation:** Compare with epigraphic records and conservation history before interpreting an unusual shape.

[Evans and Mourad, DStretch and Egyptian tomb paintings (2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X17305977)

## 8. Cueva San Borjitas (Baja California Sur, Mexico)

Documented | Painted rock art

Jon Harman's algorithm description demonstrates enhanced pictographs here, including faint color differences in overlapping figures.

**Photo to seek:** Original color images of painted panels, with enough surrounding rock to compare pigment and substrate.

**Interpretation:** This app uses regularized RGB stretching; it does not reproduce DStretch's custom YDS or YBK presets.

[Jon Harman, DStretch algorithm description and examples](https://www.dstretch.com/AlgorithmDescription.html)

## 9. Tule River Painted Rock (California, United States)

Documented | Painted rock art

The technique's author includes Tule River Painted Rock among his before-and-after rock-art demonstrations.

**Photo to seek:** Existing, permission-cleared close photographs of painted elements and their surrounding stone.

**Interpretation:** Use community-approved imagery and interpretation. Custom DStretch color-space examples may differ from this RGB implementation.

[Jon Harman, Tule River Painted Rock examples](https://www.dstretch.com/AlgorithmDescription.html)

## 10. Pyramid of Unas (Saqqara, Egypt)

Candidate | Interior inscriptions and color traces

The included image contains visibly colored inscriptions against stone. Their photographic color separation makes it a useful candidate for testing settings.

**Photo to seek:** Straight-on images of inscribed wall sections; include both colored and faint neighboring signs.

**Interpretation:** This study has not established new text or identified pigments. Shading in carved signs also changes color values.

[Aidan McRae Thomson, source photograph and metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pyramid_Texts_in_Unas%E2%80%99_Pyramid_2017.jpg)

## 11. Pompeii (Italy)

Candidate | Painted plaster and fresco details

The House of Menander example has faded wall decoration and surrounding plaster, a useful pairing for testing color differences on a selected surface.

**Photo to seek:** Close color photographs of faded painted walls, ideally with uniform light and minimal glare.

**Interpretation:** Weathering, plaster patches and conservation work can all produce distinct false colors.

[Marco Ober, House of Menander source photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_del_Menandro,_Interior,_Pompeii_(4979).jpg)

## 12. Mogao Caves (Dunhuang, China)

Candidate | Buddhist wall paintings

UNESCO documents an extensive collection of cave murals. Faded painted details are a plausible target for color enhancement; this listing does not claim documented DStretch use here.

**Photo to seek:** Licensed archival color photographs of mural details, with capture and conservation information when available.

**Interpretation:** Check whether an image depicts an original surface, restoration, reconstruction or replica.

[UNESCO, Mogao Caves](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/440/)

## 13. Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra, India)

Candidate | Buddhist mural paintings

The caves' surviving wall paintings make close photographs of faded pigment a plausible application, based on the material rather than a verified DStretch case study.

**Photo to seek:** Original color close-ups of painted wall details with consistent illumination.

**Interpretation:** Strong exposure differences and surface deterioration can overwhelm small pigment differences.

[UNESCO, Ajanta Caves](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/242/)

## 14. Bonampak (Chiapas, Mexico)

Candidate | Maya painted murals

INAH documents richly painted interior rooms and a history of conservation. Photographs of faint mural passages may be useful candidates for pigment separation.

**Photo to seek:** Permission-cleared color documentation of original mural surfaces, with dates and conservation context.

**Interpretation:** Prefer authorized archival images with conservation records and permission to make and share derivatives.

[INAH, Bonampak archaeological site](https://www.inah.gob.mx/zonas/zona-arqueologica-de-bonampak)

## 15. Tassili n'Ajjer (Algeria)

Candidate | Painted rock-art panels

UNESCO documents a major collection of rock paintings and engravings. Painted panels with faint surviving pigment are a plausible fit for this color-based technique.

**Photo to seek:** Licensed, unedited color photographs of painted panels rather than distant landscape views.

**Interpretation:** The recommendation concerns painted surfaces. Unpainted engraving geometry usually calls for lighting-based or 3D documentation instead.

[UNESCO, Tassili n'Ajjer](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/179/)

## 16. Cueva de las Manos (Santa Cruz, Argentina)

**Interpretation:** Color differences alone do not establish the sequence or dates of overlapping paintings.

[UNESCO, Cueva de las Manos](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/936/)

## 19. Great Pyramid interior (Giza, Egypt)

Exploratory | Stone surfaces

The included image demonstrates strong color differences under mixed artificial lighting. It is useful for learning the app's limits, with no new marking established.

**Photo to seek:** Well-exposed stone details under consistent lighting, ideally repeated with a color reference.

**Interpretation:** Lighting and mineral variation dominate many views. Enhanced patches do not establish paint, writing, age or human intent.

[Ovedc, Great Pyramid interior source photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:By_ovedc_-_Interior_of_the_Great_Pyramid_-_02.jpg)

## 20. Titanic wreck interior (North Atlantic)

Exploratory | Corrosion, rusticles and fixtures

NOAA's bathroom photograph is useful for exploring how corrosion and surviving surfaces separate into false colors, with much lower confidence in historical interpretation.

**Photo to seek:** Well-lit original color frames from documented expeditions; preserve the source's native resolution.

**Interpretation:** Water absorption, illumination, compression and dark-region noise dominate. This is not underwater color recovery or a way to reveal covered rooms.

[NOAA Ocean Exploration, Titanic bathtub photograph](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/edu-themes-archaeology-media-multimedia-titanic-bathtub/)

Included study: Titanic (limits) — 2,250 × 2,250 (5.1 MP). [Original source](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/edu-themes-archaeology-media-multimedia-titanic-bathtub/).

## Acquisition priorities

- Additional original-color photographs of painted Unas pyramid surfaces. Two further pyramid candidates were screened out: a monochrome Unas photograph and a Teti view with little independent color. Neither is passed off as a pigment example.
- High-resolution color records of Cliff Palace interior plaster; the available NPS comparison is useful context, but is not a native high-resolution study source.
- Iran: prioritize faded, documented original mural passages and conservation photographs over vivid repainted sections. Chehel Sotoun is a historical palace example, not prehistoric art.
- Angkor Wat: obtain an explicitly reusable original from the researcher or archive before adding it as an interactive example. The NASA article credits its comparison to Noel Hidalgo Tan; publication on NASA does not establish blanket reuse permission.
- For suspected deliberate erasure, seek dated conservation records and documented original photographs. Enhancement alone cannot identify cause, intent or an original palette.
